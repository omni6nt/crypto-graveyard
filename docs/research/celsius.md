# Celsius Network — research dossier

**Research cutoff:** 2026-11-19  
**Status:** Structured candidate; not yet approved for public release.

## Classification
Crypto lending platform collapse; founder pleaded guilty to fraud and received a prison sentence

## Core reconstruction
On June 12, 2022, Celsius halted customer withdrawals, leaving approximately $4.7 billion in customer assets inaccessible, according to DOJ. Celsius filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy on July 13, 2022. Mashinsky pleaded guilty on December 3, 2024 to commodities fraud and securities fraud, including misleading customers about Celsius's business and manipulating CEL's price while secretly selling his own holdings. On May 8, 2025, he was sentenced to 12 years in prison. Celsius's reorganization plan became effective January 31, 2024, and the company began creditor distributions and wound down its retail platform.

## Causal analysis
Analysis: Celsius combined custodial control of customer assets, high-yield promises, opaque and risky investment strategies, and a proprietary token whose market price was affected by company activity. The criminal case established that Mashinsky made material misrepresentations and participated in CEL manipulation. A rapid liquidity shock exposed the mismatch between customer expectations of access and the platform's ability to honor withdrawals. The case does not mean every yield platform operates identically; it demonstrates why custody, asset-liability management, disclosures and conflicts involving an issuer's own token matter.

## Current status and limits
Celsius's Chapter 11 plan became effective January 31, 2024. The company emerged from bankruptcy, began distributing cryptocurrency, cash and, for some creditors, equity in a reorganized mining business; its apps were shut down February 29, 2024. Stretto's distribution materials describe multiple classes and reasons some creditors' payments may be held. In the criminal case, Mashinsky pleaded guilty in December 2024 and was sentenced to 12 years in May 2025, with forfeiture of $48,393,446. An April 2026 FTC stipulated order resolved the remaining FTC matter with Mashinsky. The available sources establish ongoing distribution administration but do not support a claim that every creditor has been paid in full.

## Warning-sign discipline
Warning signs distinguish what was reasonably observable before the incident, what became visible during it, and what only became clear through later proceedings. Do not relabel hindsight evidence as a pre-incident warning.
