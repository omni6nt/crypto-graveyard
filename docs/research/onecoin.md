# OneCoin — research dossier

**Research cutoff:** 2026-11-19  
**Status:** Structured candidate; not yet approved for public release.

## Classification
Court-established international cryptocurrency pyramid scheme and fraud

## Core reconstruction
Between 2014 and 2019, OneCoin marketed packages globally and raised more than $4 billion from investors, according to DOJ. Co-founder Karl Sebastian Greenwood pleaded guilty to wire fraud and money laundering in December 2022 and was sentenced to 20 years in September 2023. Co-founder Ruja Ignatova was charged in the United States in 2017, remains at large according to the FBI's June 2026 update, and is subject to a reward of up to $5 million for information leading to her arrest and/or conviction. In April 2026, DOJ opened a remission process using more than $40 million in forfeited assets; the filing deadline passed June 30, 2026, and the administrator says no distribution date is available.

## Causal analysis
Analysis: OneCoin's growth relied on an MLM incentive structure that rewarded recruitment and package sales, while the purported cryptocurrency's technical claims were not supported by the transparent, independently verifiable market infrastructure investors would expect from an open crypto network. The DOJ's court records establish the fraud and pyramid-scheme character of the enterprise. This case should not be flattened into a generic 'bad token' story: the recruitment model, package economics, representations about the product and the lack of independent verification were central.

## Current status and limits
Greenwood pleaded guilty in December 2022 and was sentenced to 20 years in September 2023. Ruja Ignatova remains at large according to the FBI's June 25, 2026 update; the U.S. reward is up to $5 million. DOJ launched a remission process in April 2026 using more than $40 million in forfeited assets. Eligible petitioners had to have purchased OneCoin between 2014 and 2019 and experienced a net loss after accounting for completed withdrawals. The deadline was June 30, 2026; the administrator's FAQ says the filing deadline has passed and no distribution date is available. Filing does not guarantee payment.

## Warning-sign discipline
Warning signs distinguish what was reasonably observable before the incident, what became visible during it, and what only became clear through later proceedings. Do not relabel hindsight evidence as a pre-incident warning.
