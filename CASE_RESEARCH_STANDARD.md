# CRYPTO GRAVEYARD — CASE RESEARCH STANDARD

## 1. Non-negotiable principles
- Evidence before atmosphere.
- A failed project is not automatically fraudulent.
- Allegations are not findings; charges are not convictions.
- Bankruptcy is not, by itself, proof of fraud.
- A security incident does not mean the entire network permanently died.
- Community sentiment is not proof.
- Do not use hindsight to call a warning obvious if evidence was not reasonably available at the time.
- State uncertainty and disagreement.
- Every factual claim must be traceable to a source.

The Coroner may be theatrical; factual panels, timelines, labels, and examination feedback must be precise.

## 2. Claim labels
- **Documented fact:** supported by reliable records or multiple credible sources.
- **Court finding:** finding/conclusion by an identified court; state court and procedural context.
- **Official statement:** statement by company, regulator, law-enforcement body, administrator, or authority; not automatically independently verified.
- **Reported claim:** reported by a credible publication but not independently established in project materials.
- **Allegation:** accusation not established as a finding; identify source and status.
- **Analysis:** reasoned interpretation of cited evidence, not a source quotation.
- **Community reference:** community-created claim, attestation, or discussion, including Intuition; not proof.

## 3. Source hierarchy
**Tier 1 — Primary:** court judgments, indictments, filings, bankruptcy/trustee reports, regulator/law-enforcement releases, official company statements and incident reports, protocol governance records, technical postmortems, reproducible on-chain analyses.

**Tier 2 — High-quality secondary:** established news organizations with editorial standards, specialist investigative publications with transparent sourcing, credible security/forensic reports, academic papers, recognized research institutions.

**Tier 3 — Context/community:** Intuition claims and attestations, community discussions, social posts, personal blogs/commentary. Useful for leads, community perception, and additional references; not sufficient alone to establish serious allegations or legal conclusions.

## 4. Intuition handling
For each included reference, record exact public URL/stable ID, what it claims, creator/attester details if public and relevant, date/timestamp if available, object type, relevance, and a visible community-sourced label. Do not describe it as verified fact solely because it exists on Intuition; invent vote counts; add voting/attestation features; claim live integration when merely linking externally; or include an inaccessible reference.

## 5. Case dossiers
Create one dossier for FTX, Mt. Gox, Terra/LUNA, BitConnect, OneCoin, Three Arrows Capital, Celsius Network, and Thodex. Each dossier includes:

### Identity/context
What it was, stated purpose/operating model, relevant dates/jurisdictions, relevant founders/executives where properly sourced, and what users/customers/counterparties were offered.

### Event reconstruction
Dated timeline, triggering incident, sequence of material decisions/developments, and known/disputed/unknown points.

### Causal analysis
Immediate trigger, underlying vulnerabilities/contributing factors, governance/operational/technical/economic/legal dimensions as relevant, and distinction between causal evidence and later interpretation.

### Warning signs
For each: description; evidence available at the time; when visible; who could reasonably observe it; why it mattered; whether hindsight; source IDs.

### Consequences/current status
Customer impact; financial impact with definitions and dates; legal proceedings/current procedural status; bankruptcy/liquidation/restructuring/recovery/ongoing operations. Do not imply unresolved cases are concluded.

### Lessons
Practical lessons for users and builders/operators; limitations and what cannot be generalized.

### Source register
Source ID, title, publisher/issuing body, author, publication/filing date, URL, source type, claims supported, access/review date, reliability notes.

## 6. Minimum research acceptance gate
A case requires at least two independent credible sources, primary-source support for central legal/technical claims wherever available, sourced chronology, accurate classification, aftermath/current-status check, 6–8 defensible examination items, source IDs for all factual statements/events/explanations/material lessons, and final review for misleading language, unsupported numbers, and hindsight bias. Two sources are a baseline, not permission to use weak sources. Serious allegations require stronger direct evidence.

## 7. Case-specific cautions
- **FTX:** separate court-established facts, allegations, reporting, exchange and affiliated entities, executives, and proceedings; define/date financial figures; recheck bankruptcy/legal status.
- **Mt. Gox:** distinguish collapse, theft/loss events, insolvency, creditor repayment; do not imply all losses had same cause or recovery status; date distributions.
- **Terra/LUNA:** distinguish UST, LUNA, wider ecosystem, and later entities; explain mechanism accurately; separate market events, design, and legal findings; date proceedings/settlements.
- **BitConnect:** distinguish promotional claims, regulatory actions, criminal proceedings, and court outcomes; do not assign identical legal status to all promoters/participants.
- **OneCoin:** distinguish organization/promotional claims from an independently functioning blockchain; use primary legal records; do not treat every associated person as equally culpable.
- **Three Arrows Capital:** distinguish market losses, leverage, counterparty exposure, insolvency, and legal findings; verify liabilities/recovery claims.
- **Celsius Network:** distinguish products, customer liabilities, liquidity events, bankruptcy, and legal findings; date recovery/distribution status.
- **Thodex:** distinguish shutdown, founder departure/extradition, customer losses, charges, trial outcomes, appeals, and later proceedings. Do not present an earlier judgment as final current status without checking later appellate developments. Verify Turkish legal records and reliable reporting; check names/transliteration.

## 8. Writing rules
Plain English; explain technical terms; avoid sensational factual headlines; use dates; attribute claims; say “according to” when appropriate; avoid causal certainty beyond evidence; define/date financial figures and percentages; never imply endorsement by a project, protocol, court, or publisher.

## 9. Examination design
Each case has 6–8 items spanning relevant governance/control, custody/counterparty, liquidity/leverage, technical/security, incentive/economic-design, transparency/disclosure risks. Record prompt, options, defensible answer(s), explanation, source IDs, visibility (`pre_incident`, `during_incident`, `hindsight`), difficulty, and learning objective. Never ask visitors to infer guilt from suspicious behavior.

## 10. Editorial checklist
- [ ] Accurate classification.
- [ ] Central narrative supported by reliable sources.
- [ ] All material factual claims have source IDs.
- [ ] Allegations/statements/findings/analysis correctly labelled.
- [ ] Chronological sourced timeline.
- [ ] Financial figures defined and dated.
- [ ] Legal status current as of research cutoff.
- [ ] Warning signs were observable when claimed.
- [ ] Examination answers defensible and explained.
- [ ] Intuition references supplementary and labelled.
- [ ] No accusation beyond what record supports.
- [ ] Reviewed for misleading omissions and hindsight bias.
